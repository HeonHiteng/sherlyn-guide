Build a research assistant for your documents

Compare documents, trace claims to sources, and find what is missing.

NotebookLM · 2–3 documents you can upload. Allow 25–35 minutes. Account limits apply; this exercise needs only a small notebook.

1. Start with one question

Choose a specific question, such as “Which onboarding approach should our small team try?” Create a notebook in NotebookLM and add two or three related reports or PDFs. Name each source clearly with its title and year. Confirm the imported text is readable.

2. Try a tiny practice set

No documents ready? Add these as two separate pasted-text sources. They are fictional examples, not research evidence.

Source A — Team pilot, 2025
Twenty new staff tried a checklist. Median setup time was 40 minutes, compared with 60 minutes for the previous cohort. Teams were not randomly assigned. Satisfaction was not measured.
Source B — Team survey, 2026
Twelve new staff tried a video walkthrough. Nine said it was useful. Setup time was not recorded. The survey was optional and there was no comparison group.

3. Build an evidence table

Select the relevant sources and ask:

Using only the selected sources, compare the onboarding approaches in a table: approach, sample size, measured outcome, limitation, supporting citation. Write “not reported” for missing information. Separate measured findings from your interpretation. Do not rank approaches measured on different outcomes.

4. Audit the answer

Open each citation and read its supporting passage. For the practice set, checklist setup time is 40 minutes; the video study has no setup-time result. Ask “Which method improved satisfaction by 30%?” The correct response is that the sources do not establish this. A citation is a place to check, not proof that the interpretation is right.

5. Turn evidence into a decision

Suggest one small follow-up test based on these sources. State what remains unknown, which metric to measure, and what evidence would change the decision. Keep recommendations separate from source-backed facts.

Save the checked table and a short note of your own conclusion. With real papers, record study design, sample and limitations before applying the finding. If a PDF imports badly, use a readable text version and check page references against the original.
